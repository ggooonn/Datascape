The small-building plan is dense. Only 16 of 3,902 house records have native height. Start with a small sample of verified low-rise vertical information before using the plan density as a template. Compare verified, floor-derived and intentionally omitted heights for the same sites. The 3 m/floor scenario in Work 01 is a study of proportions.

## Forms and groups

- **Slab apartments**. Compare a long block with similarly aligned groups using the screened pairs. Check the actual topography and orientation before grouping them artistically.
- **Compact house and small-building masses**. Start from observed plans, roof evidence and slope. Compare compact and elongated plan shapes without inferring use.
- **Commercial and retail masses**. Compare low volumes with stepped or multipart masses where supported. Shop frontages and individual roof subdivisions require evidence or an explicit artistic designation.
- **Multipart volumes**. Test shoulders, setbacks, overhangs and shared ground contacts. Establish a policy for incomplete part coverage, overlap and near-duplicate parent records.
- **Urban edge**. Compare a dense small-footprint front, an apartment band and an open passage. Plan proximity supports a formal grouping hypothesis. Complex boundaries and ownership need independent evidence.

2. Derive visibility from the shared mapped depth and emit an artwork-mm mask with owners. The v08 project_clip pathway can consume that mask after stroke depth and owner selection. Register the metric roots and rock footprints before testing front, behind and beside relationships.
3. Keep the v08 seeded variation, contact and path provenance where applicable. Carry source-building and face IDs, face-local coordinates, material channel and phase for architecture strokes. Use visible polygons and contacts to guide placement.
4. Test a few verified roofs and facades at A. Try interrupted silhouettes, restrained vertical plane breaks, ridge or parapet lines where supported, and terrain contact strokes. Near masses can carry plane divisions. Middle masses can use silhouette, major roofline and an occasional break. Start with far omission.
5. Recover the original Spring vegetation rasters or select a documented replacement. Compare roofprint and ground footprint where source rights and imagery permit. Validate elevations in dense blocks and on steep sites. Confirm appropriate terrain and attribution for commercial artwork.
